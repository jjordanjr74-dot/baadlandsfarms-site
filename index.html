Who We Are

Safety discipline meets regenerative agriculture

Baadlands Farms is owned and operated by Stacey, a 37-year-old Amazon Safety Specialist and farm operator in Pueblo. That same rigor — hazard assessment, documented procedure, liability control — is applied to every grazing job and every product we sell.

Our livestock includes 50-plus goats (breeding bucks, does, wethers and kids), a herd of pigs, ducks, geese, laying chickens, prized roosters, turkeys and guinea fowl. We raise them on open pasture in Southern Colorado and sell direct — no middlemen, no retail markup, no mystery supply chain.

Grazing crews are moved under portable solar electric netting and every sexually intact or adult goat leaving the premises carries an official USDA Scrapie Flock ID ear tag as required by 9 CFR Part 79. We carry a $1M/$2M commercial general liability policy with an off-premises contract grazing endorsement.

Goats eat the fine fuels and ladder fuels — dry grass, cheatgrass, low brush and scrub oak sprouts — that carry a ground fire up into tree canopies. Reducing that continuous fuel bed slows fire spread and creates defensible space around structures. Unlike mowing or chipping, goats work steep and rocky ground without sparks, and they leave no herbicide residue.
